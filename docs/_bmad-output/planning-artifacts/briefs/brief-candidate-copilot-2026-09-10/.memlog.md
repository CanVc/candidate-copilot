- (event) Create intent confirmed; initialized the Candidate Copilot brief workspace from the four existing documents under docs/research.
- (decision) Current product framing: recruiter-first interactive portfolio intended mainly for post-interview exploration, with Markdown files as the source of truth.
- (decision) Public application must search only candidate-knowledge/public; private content must not be deployed to or filtered within the public runtime.
- (decision) MVP stays deliberately simple: structured Markdown, section-level lexical search, grounded answers with citations and explicit abstention; semantic/vector search only if testing proves necessary.
- (decision) The brief is an upstream planning artifact for the PRD, architecture, epics, and stories; it must establish product direction without absorbing downstream detail.
- (decision) User selected Coaching path and expects deeper requirements to be developed later in the PRD.
- (decision) The product is intentionally a live, interactive proof of work rather than a static repository link; it should both demonstrate what the candidate can build and let recruiting teams explore the candidate more deeply.
- (assumption) Recruiter demand for an interactive candidate-exploration tool is unvalidated; the brief must frame this as a hypothesis rather than an established user need.
- (decision) Before any chat interaction, the product must signal professional execution through a clean functional interface, a private session, and clear reassurance that one recruiter cannot see another recruiter's conversations.
- (decision) The intended candidate signals are end-to-end product design, architecture and development ability, documentation and methodological rigor, modernization of practices, and willingness and capacity to learn modern technologies.
- (decision) AI mastery is not a primary product claim because the implementation process and degree of AI-assisted coding remain undecided and would not be directly visible to recruiters.
- (assumption) Using an unfamiliar modern stack may turn the implementation itself into evidence of learning ability, but the stack choice is intentionally deferred to downstream architecture work.
- (decision) The landing page should not be chat-only: it should showcase selected public projects and notable experiences and use examples or suggested questions to invite exploration.
- (decision) Candidate Copilot itself may be featured as a public project; recruiters can ask the same conversational interface about its implementation, languages, architecture, and design decisions.
- (decision) Access model: a public anonymous site using only the public knowledge base, with conversations isolated per visitor rather than recruiter accounts or invitation links.
- (decision) Conversation retention is desired for product learning, must be disclosed clearly to visitors, and must never expose one visitor's conversation to another.
- (assumption) A visitor-controlled conversation deletion mechanism is likely desirable for trust and privacy, but its scope, retention period, and anonymous-session mechanics remain to be decided.
- (decision) Raw conversations will be retained for 90 days by default, with clear disclosure to visitors; manual preservation may occur but its policy and deletion implications remain unresolved.
- (decision) Retained conversations may feed a monitoring or analysis system to improve the knowledge base, reveal recruiter interests, and detect unanswered questions; this secondary use must be disclosed.
- (event) Created addendum.md to preserve downstream PRD and architecture detail about conversation retention, deletion, monitoring, and unresolved archival semantics.
- (override) Supersedes the earlier archival possibility: all raw conversations and conversation-derived records are deleted within 90 days, with earlier user-triggered deletion available; analysis occurs only inside that recruitment-cycle window.
- (decision) Conversation data is used for product and knowledge-base improvement, not to identify recruiters or enable follow-up contact.
- (change) Updated addendum.md to reflect the final 90-day deletion policy and prohibit durable anonymized conversation records.
- (event) Discovery paused at the success-criteria discussion; wrote pause-summary.md with the product framing, decisions, source list, artifact state, and exact resumption question.
- (event) Resumed the paused Coaching-path discovery from the success-criteria discussion, using pause-summary.md and the existing brief, addendum, and memlog as continuity context.
- (decision) Demonstration success means Candidate Copilot is deployed, functional, polished enough to present professionally, and used by recruiters.
- (decision) Meaningful recruiter success requires some genuine exploration through multiple questions and/or browsing; a single generic courtesy question counts only as weak or partial success.
- (decision) Exploration is conversational: users explore Candidate Copilot by asking natural-language questions; highlighted examples and presentation elements are not independently explorable content, but loose prompts that guide users toward questions.
- (decision) A meaningful engagement may be evidenced by multiple questions on distinct topics, a follow-up question, or a question inspired by a presentation cue; recruiter references in later interviews are not a practical success signal because the product is introduced around the middle or end of the interview process.
- (decision) Success threshold for recruiter utility is at least one genuinely exploratory conversation during a recruitment cycle; rates and volume targets are intentionally avoided because cycle length and interview frequency vary too much for meaningful comparison.
- (change) Drafted the Success Criteria section in brief.md using a delivery milestone and a one-session meaningful-exploration threshold, while excluding unstable rate metrics and interview progression attribution.
- (decision) The initial experience must communicate what Candidate Copilot is, what recruiters can ask, why answers are credible, conversation-data handling, the candidate profile signals being highlighted, that the candidate created the product, and an intuitive understanding of how to use it without requiring a tutorial.
- (decision) The MVP excludes motivations and desired-role matching: recruiters may arrive from companies offering materially different positions, and the anonymous public product cannot reliably reconcile answers with each visitor's originating opportunity.
- (decision) Candidate Copilot is a documentary question-answering tool, not a proxy empowered to speak or decide for the candidate: it must not guess, invent, infer undocumented intent, or make commitments on the candidate's behalf.
- (decision) Strict abstention is a core product concern: when the documentary basis is insufficient or a question requires the candidate's personal judgment, the product must say so rather than fill the gap.
- (decision) Questions framed as value judgments (for example good/bad or strong/weak) must be refused rather than answered with a verdict or silently reframed as an evidentiary assessment.
- (decision) When refusing a value judgment, Candidate Copilot should explain the boundary and offer a neutral factual direction for a new question, without automatically supplying evidence or answering the rejected premise.
- (decision) Conversation analysis and monitoring are not Candidate Copilot features; they belong to a separate project and are excluded from this product's MVP scope.
- (decision) Candidate Copilot MVP will retain conversations under the 90-day lifecycle but provide no integration with the separate future analysis project; that integration is deferred.
- (decision) During the MVP, the candidate alone may manually access retained conversations for product and knowledge-base learning; there is no automated analysis integration, and the data is not used to identify or contact recruiters.
- (decision) MVP conversation review uses controlled operational access to storage or a secure manual export; it does not include a private administration interface.
- (decision) Severe launch risks include unsupported or invented answers, prohibited value judgments, accidental private-information exposure, an interface perceived as a superficial AI gimmick, and slowness or unavailability during recruiter use.
- (decision) An exact, complete, even somewhat mechanical answer is acceptable: the recruiter's role is to form the judgment, and Candidate Copilot does not need to generate a personalized evaluative summary.
- (decision) Candidate Copilot is a personal product built around the candidate's own public documentation; becoming a reusable multi-candidate platform is not part of the product vision.
- (decision) There is no committed post-MVP roadmap; the product will be reassessed from observed use, while the underlying public documentation is expected to evolve and remain maintained.
